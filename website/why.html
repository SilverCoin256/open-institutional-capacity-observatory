Research history

Why OICO exists

Public administrative numbers are easy to quote and difficult to audit. OICO grew out of that mismatch.

The recurring problem

Workload traces are not institutions.

Pending counts, completions, complaints, filings, and accountability language can reveal parts of an administrative process. They cannot, by themselves, reveal effort, capacity, fairness, legality, intent, or causal impact. The project exists to keep those two statements visible at the same time.

Why software

A result is not reproducible if its provenance disappears.

OICO packages frozen source snapshots, checksums, schemas, transformations, metrics, figures, tests, notebooks, and limitations together. The point is not to create the largest dashboard. It is to make a claim inspectable from source row to interpretation.

What failed

The strongest decisions were demotions.

QAI was not retained as a claim of new mathematics. Later EOIR rows were excluded from the authoritative inference when their provenance and completeness could not be defended. Experimental modules were not allowed to carry the flagship claim. A pooled institutional ranking was rejected because the systems do not share a common outcome.

Next test

External researchers should try to break it.

Run the reproduction protocol, inspect the falsification invitation, and report agreement or error through the public repository. OICO is frozen for external validation, not finished because it has a website.

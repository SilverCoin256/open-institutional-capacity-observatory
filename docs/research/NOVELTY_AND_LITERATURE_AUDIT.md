# Novelty And Literature Audit

Audit date: 2026-08-10. This is a calibration record, not a claim of priority.

## Surviving contribution

OICO's defensible contribution is an integrated, low-dependency, open research package that combines frozen public-data snapshots, source and license metadata, a canonical schema, transparent descriptive metrics, bounded simulation modules, deterministic figures, executable notebooks, benchmark formats, clean-room checks, and explicit misuse boundaries. The EOIR case supplies a reproducible demonstration of that workflow.

The formula called QAI is not claimed as a new statistic. It is algebraically a normalized change in a stock relative to a flow. The project earns credibility by stating that plainly.

## Prior-work calibration

- The official [EOIR workload and adjudication statistics](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/workload-and-adjudication-statistics) publish pending cases, receipts, and completions, and warn that staff update the case database. OICO therefore treats the series as a versioned snapshot rather than an immutable ground truth.
- The [GAO review of EOIR](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-438) already documented backlog growth, completion trends, management challenges, and workforce planning. OICO cannot claim to have discovered the existence of the backlog.
- Kerwin and Kerwin's [2024 staffing-needs analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/23315024241226645) models judge-team requirements under alternative receipt and completion scenarios. OICO is narrower and descriptive; it does not compete with that staffing model.
- Bednar's [agency capacity measure](https://doi.org/10.1017/psrm.2023.27) uses more than 190 million personnel records to construct agency-year capacity scores. OICO's public workload trace is much smaller and measures a different observable.
- Bach et al.'s [burden-capacity-gap study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/bureaucratic-quality-and-the-gap-between-implementation-burden-and-administrative-capacities/D4F1B8007FCA4753D2803F3EFC8A84A9) combines multiple data sources in a latent-variable framework and performs validation and robustness checks. OICO should not present its single-series ratio as equivalent.
- Williams' [critique of broad state-capacity language](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1744137420000478) is directly relevant: hypothetical capacity, observable performance, and implementation conditions should not be collapsed into one score.

## Verdict by contribution type

| Contribution type | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| New mathematical index | Weak / rejected | QAI is a transparent normalization of familiar workload quantities. |
| New EOIR empirical fact | Weak | Backlog growth and completion pressure are already documented by government, GAO, and scholarship. |
| Causal explanation | Absent | No identification strategy, intervention, or outcome validation. |
| Open measurement infrastructure | Moderate and credible | The integrated provenance, reproduction, benchmark, and misuse-boundary package is useful if maintained and independently tested. |
| Teaching and methods artifact | Moderate | Small, inspectable data and explicit proxy limitations make it suitable for critique-oriented instruction. |
| Benchmark resource | Early-stage only | Existing tasks have small samples, proxy labels, or synthetic parameters; no public leaderboard is warranted. |

## Required future novelty test

To claim a stronger scientific contribution, a successor study must add at least two independent institutions or jurisdictions, a preregistered construct definition, a transparent comparison against established capacity measures, uncertainty quantification, and an external validation target. A new label or larger dashboard is insufficient.

# OICO Study 1 Results

Status: exploratory descriptive study. Machine-readable results: `research/outputs/study1_results.json`.

## Research question

Do standardized workload signals reveal consistent processing pressure within multiple public administrative systems beyond the information in simpler conventional measures?

## Data

| System | Frequency | N | Primary variables |
|---|---|---:|---|
| EOIR | annual | 9 authoritative periods | pending, received, completions |
| USCIS | quarterly | 12 | pending, completions |
| CFPB | monthly | 168 | volume, operational rate, substantive rate |
| SEC | annual | 19 | filing volume, review intensity, response intensity |

## Results

- EOIR: QAI was positive in 8 of 8 authoritative transitions, with mean 1.095125. QAI and percentage backlog growth had Pearson r = 0.748307; the sign agreement shows that QAI adds normalization, not a different direction of evidence.
- USCIS: QAI was positive in 11 of 11 transitions, with mean 0.016438. QAI and percentage backlog growth had Pearson r = 0.984378.
- CFPB: annual volume and substantive rate had r = 0.690828; monthly r = 0.650484. This association is descriptive and may reflect reporting, demand, composition, or time trends.
- SEC: filing volume and review intensity had r = 0.841144, while filing volume and response intensity had r = -0.097657. The different associations illustrate why one generic congestion score would be misleading.

## Interpretation

Queue measures agree in direction within the two immigration systems, but they are not evidence of the same capacity construct. CFPB and SEC indicators show that volume, operational response, substantive outcomes, and review activity can move differently. The study therefore supports a comparative workflow and a warning against pooled ranking, not a universal institutional-capacity index.

## Uncertainty and limitation

No inferential confidence intervals are reported for the small aggregate queue panels. The associations are not causal. No independent waiting-time, case-age, staffing, or service-outcome series is linked to the same observations. Source retrieval is inherited and not fully recoverable.

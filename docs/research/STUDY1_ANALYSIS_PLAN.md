# OICO Study 1 Analysis Plan

Final plan written: 2026-08-10, before the final Study 1 output was regenerated. An earlier exploratory run existed and is not treated as preregistration.

## Research question

Do standardized workload signals reveal consistent processing pressure within multiple public administrative systems beyond the information in simpler conventional measures?

## Scope and units

- EOIR: annual 11-row frozen artifact window, with FY2016-FY2024 as the authoritative inference window (9 periods and 8 transitions). FY2025 and FY2026 remain stored for audit but are excluded from the primary inference.
- USCIS: quarterly FY2019Q1-FY2021Q4 snapshot, 12 periods and 11 transitions.
- CFPB: monthly 2012-01 through 2025-12, 168 periods; annual aggregation is descriptive.
- SEC: annual 2006-2024, 19 periods.
- No pooled score across institutions. Reporting units and constructs are not interchangeable.

## Primary comparisons

1. QAI versus absolute pending change, percentage backlog growth, clearance ratio, and backlog/completions for queue series.
2. Volume versus operational and substantive rates for CFPB.
3. Filing volume versus review and response intensity for SEC.
4. Sign agreement, within-series Pearson association, trend slope, and EOIR leave-one-transition-out sensitivity.

## Exclusions

- EOIR FY2025/FY2026 are excluded from the primary window because they are later partial/revised publication states and the live EOIR publication is mutable; FY2026 is incomplete in the preserved artifact.
- Missing completion values do not become zero.
- Unlike institutions are not ranked or pooled.
- No causal, predictive, or ground-truth validation claim is planned.

## Success and failure criteria

The study succeeds as a measurement audit if conventional signals can be reproduced and their overlap/non-overlap is made explicit. It fails as a general capacity validation if no independent outcome is available, if definitions are not comparable, or if the result cannot distinguish workload pressure from reporting artifacts. Those failures must be reported, not repaired rhetorically.

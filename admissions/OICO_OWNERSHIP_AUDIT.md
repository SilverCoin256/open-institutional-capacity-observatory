# OICO Intellectual Ownership Audit

This is a factual preparation document for explaining the work to a skeptical professor. It is not a claim that every line was written without AI assistance.

## Original problem

Public administrative traces can show workload, but they are fragmented, definition-sensitive, and easy to overinterpret as institutional quality.

## Scientific decisions

- Freeze source snapshots rather than silently update live endpoints.
- Keep source, derived data, code, figures, and claims in one audit path.
- Separate stable, beta, and experimental evidence.
- Compare QAI with conventional baselines.
- Refuse pooled cross-institution rankings without shared outcomes.
- Exclude incomplete/unrecoverable later EOIR rows from the authoritative claim.

## Rejected ideas

- A claim that QAI is new mathematics.
- A claim that backlog growth proves staffing failure or institutional collapse.
- A universal institutional-capacity score.
- A public leaderboard using proxy or synthetic labels.
- Claims of lab use, citation, course adoption, DOI, or conference impact without evidence.

## Trivial versus genuinely useful

The arithmetic of QAI is trivial. The useful work is making the data lineage, assumptions, failure modes, alternative measures, and reproduction contract inspectable. That usefulness remains a hypothesis until an outside researcher uses it.

## AI use

Generative AI assisted repository inspection, standards research, drafting, and implementation during the finalization workflow. Human responsibility remains with the project author for data choices, interpretation, licensing, disclosure, and verification.

## Remaining uncertainty

The central construct has no independent waiting-time, staffing, or outcome validation. An independent researcher could show that the observed signals are reporting artifacts or not useful beyond conventional measures.

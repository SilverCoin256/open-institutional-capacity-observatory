# OICO Explanations

## One sentence

OICO is open research software that measures observable administrative backlog and oversight pressure from reproducible public data while making clear what those measurements cannot prove.

## 30 words

I built OICO, open software that turns public workload and governance records into reproducible indicators of review pressure, with explicit limits against confusing proxies with institutional quality.

## 75 words

OICO studies what happens when institutions review and oversee more decisions than their public records suggest they can process. I built a Python package, data pipeline, tests, figures, benchmarks, and website around a flagship EOIR workload analysis. Its stable indicator measures backlog growth relative to recorded completions. The project also labels less-validated models as experimental, publishes provenance and checksums, and gives independent researchers an exact reproduction protocol.

## 150 words

OICO began with a recurring measurement problem: researchers studying administrative systems and AI governance often have public traces but no transparent, reusable way to connect workload, review, and accountability. I built an open research platform around a narrow question: when recorded decision volume grows faster than recorded throughput, what can be observed without claiming to measure institutional quality? The flagship EOIR case reproduces a descriptive backlog-pressure indicator from frozen public workload data. The repository includes source provenance, license decisions, schemas, deterministic transformations, tests, figures, notebooks, benchmarks, a CLI, a static research site, and a clean-room replication protocol. I also changed the project hierarchy after adversarial review: one indicator is stable, a document corpus is beta, and three formal models are experimental until calibration exists. The result is a usable scientific artifact with a clear boundary between a public-data signal and a causal claim.

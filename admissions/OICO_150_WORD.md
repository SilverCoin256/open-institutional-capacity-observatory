I built the Open Institutional Capacity Observatory, a public Python research package for asking what administrative workload data can support without confusing an observable proxy with institutional capacity. The package freezes public-data snapshots, records provenance and licensing caveats, recomputes transparent metrics, validates schemas and outputs, and exposes the analysis through a CLI, notebooks, figures, benchmark pilots, and a clean-room reproduction path. In the authoritative EOIR case, pending matters increased from 826,488 in FY2016 to 3,925,351 in FY2024, a 4.749-fold increase; a normalized backlog-change signal was positive in all eight comparable transitions. The result is descriptive, not causal: it does not show staffing failure, legal noncompliance, intent, or AI impact. The most important part of the project is the correction itself. After auditing prior work, I demoted the named formula from a claimed new index to a transparent normalization and froze broader claims until an independent researcher validates the source and construct.
